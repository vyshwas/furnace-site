Why Furnace exists

AI output looks finished. It often is not.

AI-generated software can look finished and still ship broken journeys, accessibility issues, design drift, or regressions.

Furnace checks the parts most likely to slip through. It runs your app in a real browser, verifies what the code actually does, and hands every result to one of four outcomes — proven, repaired, human required, or unknown.

Outcomes

Four answers, no hedging

Proven

Checked against your rules and passing. Nothing found that breaks them.

Repaired

Fixed automatically, then re-checked by a separate verifier — with a receipt as proof.

Human required

Login screens, visual judgment calls, and anything Furnace cannot decide alone. Flagged, never guessed.

Unknown

Could not be proven either way. Listed openly, never marked as passing.

No false confidence. Furnace does not call something safe just because a test ran. If it cannot prove something, it says unknown.

How it works

Five steps, one receipt

01

Point Furnace at your app

Tell it which pages to check. Setup takes minutes and nothing leaves your machine.

02

Furnace checks the change and its blast radius

Pages are verified in a real browser, and earlier fixes are re-checked so a new change cannot quietly regress them.

03

Safe issues are repaired

Only fixes that can be proven safe. Everything else is left alone for a human.

04

A separate verifier checks the fix again

The fixer and the checker are independent, so nothing grades its own homework.

05

You get a receipt showing what was proven and what was not

Passes, repairs, open questions, and unknowns — in one file you can attach to a pull request.

Under the hood

What the engine does

01

Headless Browser Verification

Furnace boots native Chromium and inspects the page the way assistive technology sees it. It verifies computed contrast against resolvable background colors, physical bounding-box touch targets, and keyboard exit controls in dialog markup. Gradients, unresolvable backgrounds, and live trap behavior are reported unknown.

02

Fixes are checked, then saved as patch files

Furnace rewrites the component's syntax tree in memory. A separate verifier then re-checks the changed file: it must still parse, the original problem must be gone, and no new problem may appear. Anything that fails is thrown away. Accepted fixes are written as patch files for you to review; your source files are never edited. This check works on the file itself: the page is not re-rendered in a browser during it.

03

Objective Compliance Signals Pass

Lightweight static and runtime detection for 5 objective compliance vectors: third-party font calls, tracking scripts before consent, email unsubscribe mechanisms, subscription checkout renewal disclosures, and user-generated content takedown routes.

04

Receipts & Unknowns Ledger

Every repair gets a receipt. Its goal is fingerprinted with SHA-256 before the fixer starts, so the fixer cannot quietly lower the bar. The fingerprint shows whether a receipt was edited; it is not a signature and does not prove who ran it. Surfaces that cannot be definitively proven (e.g. unauthenticated portals or dynamic canvas) are listed as unknown and routed to humans.

Measured

What we have actually measured

Run time One repair run on a sample component, Windows laptop
~10 s

Wall-clock time including Node start-up. Browser checks on real pages take longer and depend on your app. No production benchmarks yet.

Fixes that add a problem A rule, not a measured rate
Rejected

The verifier throws away any proposed fix that introduces a new finding. The rule is covered by tests; we have not yet measured it across many real projects.

What leaves your machine Checked against the code
No telemetry

Runs send nothing to us and your code is never uploaded. Furnace only contacts the pages you tell it to check; installing downloads packages from npm and a copy of Chrome.

You run it
